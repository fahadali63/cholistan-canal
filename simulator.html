What the paper finds

Six governance gaps were scored from 5 (sound) to 1 (failing). Four failed and two were weak. The water results below come from this model with its default settings.

The paper proposes a sequenced reset: treat spending so far as sunk, settle the Kotri minimum and publish ten-daily data, make consent a condition of approval, rebaseline the scope and cost, fund drinking water separately, and build in phases sized to proven water.

How the simulation works

  1. River record. Annual Sutlej flow at Sulemanki and annual flow below Kotri for each year come from IRSA. They are spread over 36 ten-day periods using a typical monsoon-heavy pattern.
  2. Surplus. Water that would reach the sea above the minimum flow is the only water nobody downstream uses. Every cusec taken upstream is about one cusec less below Kotri.
  3. Rule. The chosen rule sets the canal's legal draw. The canal head (4,120 cusecs) and the Sutlej plus the added link capacity set its physical limit.
  4. Whose water. The draw is taken first from the surplus, then from Punjab's own canals if allowed, then from the delta minimum, then from other provinces' supplies.
  5. Crop. Demand is FAO-56 crop water use for the chosen crop, less rain, divided by field efficiency and distribution losses.
1 cusec = 0.0283 m³/s 4,120 cusecs = 116.7 m³/s (design) 1 cusec for 1 day = 1.9835 acre-feet 1 MAF = 1.233 km³ 1 mm/day on 1 acre = 0.001654 cusecs

Assumptions and limits

  • Monthly pattern is modelled. IRSA publishes annual and seasonal totals, not ten-day flows. The split across months is an assumption fitted to IRSA's Kharif and Rabi averages.
  • Canal routes are schematic. No alignment drawing is public. The route follows the reported Sulemanki to Fort Abbas line and a westward Marot branch.
  • 2025-26 is partly estimated. Kharif 2025 flow below Kotri (30.85 MAF) is actual. Rabi is taken as the five-year average, and the Sutlej share of the 14.5 MAF eastern-river inflow is estimated at 8 MAF.
  • Floods are averaged. A ten-day mean hides peaks. Real canals often close at peak flood to keep silt out, so flood-year results are a best case.
  • No groundwater. Cholistan's groundwater is mostly brackish (GWP), so the model assumes the canal is the only source.
  • Accord shares only. The model uses the Accord's para 4 flood split of 37%, 37%, 14% and 12%. It does not settle the legal dispute.
